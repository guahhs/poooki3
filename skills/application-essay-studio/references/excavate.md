# Excavate mode

For applicants with no draft, or with a draft whose material is too thin to
review. The output is **questions and the applicant's own answers written down** —
never prose, and never a suggested topic.

## The operating assumption

Everyone has usable material, and it surfaces late. An applicant who says after
twenty minutes that nothing interesting has ever happened to them is at the
expected point in the process, not at a dead end. Keep going past it. This is the
single most important thing about this mode: the first round of apparent emptiness
is not a signal to start proposing topics.

The failure mode to avoid is supplying a subject. The moment this mode suggests
"you could write about your grandmother", the applicant's own material stops
arriving and they begin performing an assignment.

## Before the interview

Offer the two things that work without any help:

- **Freewrite with no word limit and no prompt.** Forget it is an application
  essay. The constraint is what blocks people, so remove it first.
- **Journal for fifteen minutes a day for several days**, across the last few
  years of their life. Breadth of recall beats depth of effort here.

Material that arrives this way is usually better than material produced under
interview, so ask whether they will do this before scheduling questions.

## The interview

Work through these in order, recording answers verbatim. Verbatim matters:
their phrasing is the voice sample that later drafting will need.

**Concrete incidents.** Not themes. Ask for moments with a time and a place.
- When did something go badly wrong, and what did you do in the following week?
- When were you nearly stopped or excluded from something you wanted?
- What did you do repeatedly that nobody asked you to do?
- When did you change your mind about something that mattered?
- What is the most recent thing you learned that was hard to learn?

**Ordinary detail.** The strongest material is usually unremarkable on its face
and reveals something only when examined. Ask about routines, small
responsibilities, things they are unreasonably particular about, what they do
when waiting. Do not hunt for an impressive subject.

**Process over outcome.** Where an achievement exists, the achievement is not the
material — the method is.
- You said you worked twelve hours a day. What did you actually *do* in those
  hours, and what did you stop doing that had not worked?
- What does your best hour of studying look like, specifically?
- What would you tell someone starting where you started?

Plenty of applicants work hard. Almost none can describe *how* they work. The
describable method is what is not substitutable.

**The subject itself.** For academic and fit essays, the material is partly
intellectual, and this is where thin drafts are usually thin.
- What specific problem inside your field do you actually want to work on?
- What is the hardest unsolved part of it?
- Who is working on it, and where?
- What does someone who disagrees with you say?
- Where do you want to be in ten years, concretely enough to be wrong about?

If they cannot answer these, the next step is reading, not writing. Supply source
URLs and come back. An essay cannot be edited into expertise.

## Closing the session

Two tests before leaving this mode:

- **Three things.** List what a reader would now know about this applicant. Fewer
  than three distinct things means the material is not yet enough for an essay.
- **Substitution.** For each candidate thread, ask how many other applicants from
  a similar background could write the same thing. The thread that survives is
  the one to develop.

Then hand back:

- the recorded answers, in their own words,
- two or three candidate threads with what each would need to become an essay,
- the specific gaps — what they would have to find out, do, or decide first,
- **no recommendation of which thread to use.** Choosing is theirs; the whole
  point of this mode is that the subject comes from them.

## Where this mode ends

When a candidate thread has a concrete incident, a describable method or insight,
and a link to what they want to do next, there is enough to draft against. Move
to `restructure` for a spine, not straight to prose.
